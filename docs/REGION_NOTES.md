# Region notes: detailed history (moved from PROJECT_NOTES.md; CLAUDE.md is the short current version)

## What this is
An interactive map of a MUD ("Disgruntled Realms", SMAUG), built from the owner's TinTin++ map file (`src/world`). A Python pipeline paints terrain from room layouts and writes one big SVG page (sidebar of areas with directions from Darkhaven Square, markers, route highlighting). The owner gives directions, arranges layouts in editors, and reviews pictures. Version 9 (Oct 9, 2026) is the current state.

Mapped: New Darkhaven, Forest of Tears + Western Trade Route, Miden'nir, Shadow Grove, Forgotten Woods (+ Lake Tich'Pyga), Ashen Forest + Eastern Trade Route, Northern Trade Route (as far as its long bridge), and sub-areas off those (City of Salburg, Keep of Belial, Wolf's Den, King's Castle, Ocean Keep, Moria, etc.).
NOT mapped yet: Barren Peaks, Northern Plains, and everything beyond; Castle Darkhawk (owner has not given directions); Mountains of Desolation proper, Azure Sea coast, King's Castle proper, Moria proper; all other undrawn areas. The map is expected to grow to about 4x its current area.

## THE NEW PHASE: why, and what to build
Problem: the page downloads small (709 KB, 212 KB gzipped) but is slow to RUN: about 13,400 SVG elements (9,700 trees, 1,200 tufts, 250 trail lines, ground shapes); zooming ran about 136 ms/frame (headless Chromium, software rendering). At 4x the map that is over 50,000 elements: unusable. The page will go on a real website and must be fast, including on phones.
Decision (agreed with owner): **bake the terrain into image tiles; keep markers, names, search, cards and route highlighting as a light live layer** (Leaflet with L.CRS.Simple, or MapLibre). Static hosting, no server.
Sizing (owner's requirements): maximum zoom only needs Darkhaven plus its surrounding trail (about 27 x 26 rooms) to fit one view on a 1920x1080 monitor, about 33 pixels per room (24 on a 1366 laptop). Minimum zoom: whole map in about 512 px.
- Today's map: 194 x 128 rooms: about 6,400 x 4,200 px at the deepest level, 5 levels, about 610 tiles of 256 px.
- 4x map: about 12,800 x 8,400 px, 6 levels, about 2,300 tiles, roughly 60 MB as WebP; one screen loads about 1 MB. High-DPI screens want 2x tiles (4x as many); consider 2x for only the top one or two levels.
- Owner does not mind build time; credits per change are the first problem.

### First task: tile proof of concept (do this in the first chat; keep it small)
1. Confirm the build: `cd src && python3 build.py` (should match the shipped `realm_map.html` except the footer).
2. Write a renderer that loads the built page in headless Chromium (Playwright), lets the label placement run, then hides markers/pins and name labels (keep the `#gfx` drawings, which are positioned from the labels), and screenshots the terrain layer at the deepest scale in chunks (about 2000 px squares), then slices into 256 px tiles (pyvips or PIL) as WebP in a z/x/y folder. Decide whether the big area names ("The Forgotten Woods", etc.) are baked or live.
3. Write a minimal Leaflet page (CRS.Simple, world-room units mapped to pixels) with the tiles plus the markers/names from the page's own data written to small JSON files (places, split per region later).
4. Measure against the current page: first load, tiles per screen, frame time while panning/zooming, on desktop and (if possible) a phone-sized viewport. Report in a short table.
5. Stop and show the owner before converting the pipeline. If good: change only the final step of the pipeline (from "write one huge page" to "render tiles + write data files"), port the sidebar (search, cards, route highlight, legend) to the live layer.

## How the page is organised (for the tile work)
`template.html` builds a string `WILD` of SVG layers in order: land, ground tints, shallows, sand, mazes, trees/tufts, trails and roads (`FWPATHS`, roads via `ROADLAYERS`), maze names, big area names; then `#gfx` (castle, windmill, town, inn, keep drawings and small text labels), `#routeLayer` (the selected route, live), `#pins` (markers + names). Labels are placed by a solver (`placeLabels`), with hand-placed names in the `LBL` table and the `PINNED` set; `NUDGE` moves a few markers. Marker kinds: dot, guild diamond, landmark square, town emblem (Ofcol, City of Salburg), mob. World units are "rooms"; north is negative y.

## Gotchas (bugs we already hit; don't repeat them)
1. Never fill "all open water west of X" without a bounding box: an unbounded fill once covered the whole sea west of the Woods (2,300 sq rooms). After ANY coast change, diff the land against the last snapshot and list the new pieces.
2. Re-contouring the land from a grid shifts coastlines by a few specks; harmless, but check.
3. Any change to the land mask must also clip the ground-tint layers and clear trees/tufts in the cut area, or you get tinted blobs over water.
4. A route's marker belongs at the room where the route leaves the area (trim the traced route there), not at the end of the drawn rooms.
5. Roads must be drawn layer by layer across ALL segments (no per-segment layers), or seams show at joints.
6. Lists like `w['gfx']` are appended to, never replaced.
7. After placing anything, check numerically (distance to road, to water, overlaps with names) before taking a screenshot; the pale shallow-water band can look like land in pictures.
8. The label solver check currently flags 11 hand-placed names as "touching"; that is the expected baseline.
9. Fonts: the page uses Alegreya from Google Fonts, which the sandbox cannot reach. `site_build.py` embeds `src/fonts/` (the same faces, from Fontsource) and runs the page script only after they load, so the bake and the label solver match what a visitor sees. Doing this moved 13 solver-placed names slightly (hand-placed names unaffected); screenshots taken without the fonts show the old placement.
11. Lossless WebP at maximum effort (quality 100, method 6) is far too slow for 600+ tiles; `site_build.py` uses lossless effort 60, method 4 (same pixels).
10. Tile seams: at fractional zooms Leaflet leaves faint lines between tiles; the page therefore snaps to whole zoom levels (zoomSnap 1). `mix-blend-mode: plus-lighter` made seams worse in Chromium here; enlarging tiles did not help either.
